# 74HC373

The 74HC373 is an octal transparent latch used to hold the lower address byte stable while the 8031 shares the same bus lines for address and data phases.

Note: the 74HC373 is functionally identical to the 74HC573 (both are octal
transparent D-latches with 3-state outputs), but it uses the classic 74x373
**interleaved** pinout — the D inputs and Q outputs alternate down each side,
rather than being grouped on opposite sides as on the 74HC573. This board uses
the 373 pin arrangement.

## Pinout

```text
                                  TOP VIEW
                                 +----U----+
             OE# (Output Enable) | 1    20 | VCC (+5V)
                     Q0 (Output 0)| 2    19 | Q7 (Output 7)
                     D0 (Data 0)  | 3    18 | D7 (Data 7)
                     D1 (Data 1)  | 4    17 | D6 (Data 6)
                     Q1 (Output 1)| 5    16 | Q6 (Output 6)
                     Q2 (Output 2)| 6    15 | Q5 (Output 5)
                     D2 (Data 2)  | 7    14 | D5 (Data 5)
                     D3 (Data 3)  | 8    13 | D4 (Data 4)
                     Q3 (Output 3)| 9    12 | Q4 (Output 4)
                     GND (0V)     | 10   11 | LE (Latch Enable)
                                 +---------+

```

## Notes

- The actual device pins are labeled as `D0`-`D7` on the data (input) side and `Q0`-`Q7` on the latched output side. On the 74HC373 these are interleaved: Q outputs sit on pins 2, 5, 6, 9, 12, 15, 16, 19 and D inputs on pins 3, 4, 7, 8, 13, 14, 17, 18.
- The 8031 P0 (AD0-AD7) bus drives the D inputs; the Q outputs drive the EPROM/SRAM low address lines A0-A7.
- The board wiring uses this latch as the address/data bus storage stage between the 8031, EPROM, and SRAM.
- The latch keeps data stable during external memory cycles and the address decode window.

## Reverse-engineering relevance

This latch is one of the most important parts of the firmware mapping layer. It is the device that freezes the lower address byte while the 8031 places data on the same bus lines.

In practice, that means:

- the CPU can share `P0` for address and data without bus contention;
- `ALE` starts the latch window;
- the active memory device is then selected by the decoded address block;
- firmware reads and writes become traceable to specific memory windows and chip-select events.

This is the hardware mechanism that makes the binary file inspectable as a board-level memory image rather than a standalone CPU program.
