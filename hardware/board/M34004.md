# M34004

The M34004 is a quad op-amp used as the board's analog/serial interface conditioning stage, effectively acting as a level shifter and signal translator between TTL logic and the higher-voltage serial domain. (M34004 is the board marking for the Motorola MC34004 quad op-amp.)

## Pinout

```text
             +----U----+
   OUTPUT 1  | 1    14 | OUTPUT 4
    INPUT 1- | 2    13 | INPUT 4-
    INPUT 1+ | 3    12 | INPUT 4+
      VCC+   | 4    11 | VCC-
    INPUT 2+ | 5    10 | INPUT 3+
    INPUT 2- | 6     9 | INPUT 3-

             +---------+
```

## Board connections

```text
                                  +----U----+
               DB9 pin 3 (RXD) <- | 1    14 | -> DB9 pin 5 (GND)
    8031 pin 11 (TXD) -> 100kΩ -> | 2    13 | <- 100kΩ <- 8031 pin 15
                  pin 12 (linked) | 3────12 | linked through 22kΩ to DB9 pin 9 (cut)
                         +9V      | 4    11 | <- MAX1044 -9V
                      GND -> 1MΩ  | 5    10 | <- 1MΩ <- GND
            connection to pin 7 ──| 6┐   ┌9 |── connected to pin 8
             ADC pin 3 <- 10kΩ <- | 7┘   └8 | -> 10kΩ -> ADC pin 4
                                  +---------+
```

## Notes

- This IC is used as the analog/serial signal translator and line conditioning block.
- The design uses the op-amp array to convert the microcontroller's TTL logic into the board's RS-232-like signal levels.

### 1. Why that line was cut

Your board was originally designed to be a universal interface. The designer connected the Op-Amps' reference line (Pins 3 and 12) through a resistor to DB9 Pin 9 so that an external device could physically inject a custom reference voltage into your board over the serial cable.

However, in standard computer serial ports, DB9 Pin 9 is usually left empty or unused. Because no voltage was coming from the computer, the line was deliberately cut to disconnect it, allowing you to tie that reference point somewhere stable instead.

### 2. How to fix the cut line

Since that line is cut, the Non-Inverting inputs (Pin 3 and Pin 12) of your M34004 (Motorola MC34004 equivalent) are currently floating mid-air. This will cause your serial data to become completely scrambled or stop transmitting altogether.

To make the circuit work, you must solder a small jumper wire from that combined Pin 3 / Pin 12 junction directly to one of two places:
- Option A (Most Common): Solder a wire from Pins 3/12 straight to Circuit Ground (0V). This gives the Op-Amp a 0V baseline. When the 8031 sends a 5V logic pulse, the Op-Amp swings to +9V. When the 8031 sends 0V, the Op-Amp swings to -9V, creating a perfect RS232 signal.
- Option B: Solder it to a +2.5V reference line if your board has a voltage divider.
