# 74HC573

The 74HC573 is an octal transparent latch used to hold the lower address byte stable while the 8031 shares the same bus lines for address and data phases.

## Pinout

```text
                                  TOP VIEW
                                 +----U----+
             OE# (Output Enable) | 1    20 | VCC (+5V)
                     D0 (Data 0) | 2    19 | Q0 (Output 0)
                     D1 (Data 1) | 3    18 | Q1 (Output 1)
                     D2 (Data 2) | 4    17 | Q2 (Output 2)
                     D3 (Data 3) | 5    16 | Q3 (Output 3)
                     D4 (Data 4) | 6    15 | Q4 (Output 4)
                     D5 (Data 5) | 7    14 | Q5 (Output 5)
                     D6 (Data 6) | 8    13 | Q6 (Output 6)
                     D7 (Data 7) | 9    12 | Q7 (Output 7)
                     GND (0V)    | 10   11 | LE (Latch Enable)
                                 +---------+

```

## Board connections

```text
                                  TOP VIEW
                                  +----U----+
                            GND ──| 1    20 |── VCC
                    EPROM pin 4 ──| 2    19 |── EPROM pin 3
      EPROM pin 18, 8031 pin 33 ──| 3    18 |── EPROM pin 19
      EPROM pin 16, 8031 pin 35 ──| 4    17 |── 8031 pin 34
                    EPROM pin 6 ──| 5    16 |── EPROM pin 5
                    EPROM pin 8 ──| 6    15 |── EPROM pin 7
      EPROM pin 13, 8031 pin 37 ──| 7    14 |── 8031 pin 36, EPROM pin 15
      EPROM pin 12, 8031 pin 38 ──| 8    13 |── 8031 pin 39, EPROM pin 11
                    EPROM pin 9 ──| 9    12 |── EPROM pin 10, SRAM pin 10
                            GND ──| 10   11 |── 8031 pin 30
                                  +---------+
```

## Notes

- The actual device pins are labeled as `D0`-`D7` on the data side and `Q0`-`Q7` on the latched output side.
- The board wiring uses this latch as the address/data bus storage stage between the 8031, EPROM, and SRAM.
- The latch keeps data stable during external memory cycles and the address decode window.

## Reverse-engineering relevance

This latch is one of the most important parts of the firmware mapping layer. It is the device that freezes the lower address byte while the 8031 places data on the same bus lines.

In practice, that means:

- the CPU can share `P0` for address and data without bus contention;
- `ALE` starts the latch window;
- the active memory device is then selected by the decoded address block;
- firmware reads and writes become traceable to specific memory windows and chip-select events.

This is the hardware mechanism that makes the binary file inspectable as a board-level memory image rather than a standalone CPU program.
